Agentic AI

Towards a Science of AI Agent Reliability

Princeton University (Rabanser, Kapoor, Kirgis, Liu, Utpala, Narayanan) — accepted ICML 2026published 2026-02paper40 mindeep

A single accuracy score hides four separate things that can go wrong: the same agent giving different answers to the identical task, breaking when instructions are reworded but not changed in meaning, not knowing when it's wrong, and occasionally failing in unbounded, severe ways. Across 18 months of frontier model releases, raw accuracy improved much faster than any of these four measures did — meaning newer, higher-scoring models aren't necessarily more trustworthy, just more capable.

Log analysis is necessary for credible evaluation of AI agents

Princeton University, UK AISI, Apollo Research, Transluce, Meridian Labs, UC Berkeley (Kirgis, Kapoor, Rabanser, Narayanan et al.)published 2026-05paper25 minworking

Researchers re-examined a standard customer-service agent benchmark by reading the actual execution logs instead of trusting the pass/fail score, and found the benchmark had undersold the agent's real capability by nearly 50% because of flawed test tasks. In the same logs they also found the agent taking risky financial actions — wrong refund amounts, wrong payment methods — that the pass/fail number never surfaced at all.

AI in Banking

Sound Practices for Responsible Adoption of Artificial Intelligence (AI): Consultation report

Financial Stability Boardpublished 2026-06docs30 minworking

The first international supervisory framework aimed specifically at agentic AI in banks is built on named case studies rather than abstract principles. Its flagship example: a globally systemic bank built an in-house fraud-detection agent in three months that watches over 80 million signals a day and drafts new detection rules — but every single rule it proposes still goes to a human analyst for approval before going live. Regulators hold this up as the model to follow, while noting plainly that manual approval of every action won't hold up once agent activity keeps growing.

Read if you're in risk or compliance conversations with a bank; skip if you're not selling into regulated financial services.

Summary of AI roundtables - February 2026

Bank of Englandpublished 2026-02docs8 minintro

In closed-door sessions, UK challenger banks, global banks, and insurers told the Bank of England the same thing: they don't want new AI-specific regulation or a dedicated AI sandbox. They believe the existing principles-based supervisory framework, particularly the rules already governing model risk management, gives them enough room to deploy AI responsibly.
