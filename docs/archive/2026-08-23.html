Three unconnected but useful data points today: a concrete lesson on how a real sales-research agent breaks and gets fixed in production, a hard regulatory deadline forcing EU banks to fold AI-driven cyber risk into existing resilience programs, and a core-banking platform vendor turning agent governance into a built-in feature for banks too small to build it themselves.

Agentic AI

Production-Ready AI Agents: 5 Lessons from Refactoring a Monolith

Google Developers Blogpublished 2026-04article15 minworking

A single-script sales-research agent kept silently stalling because one failed sub-task took down the whole pipeline with no clear failure point. The fix wasn't a better model — it was breaking the monolith into narrow, independently-testable sub-agents (a Company Researcher, a Case Study Researcher, a Selector, an Email Drafter) each responsible for one job, plus circuit breakers that cap runaway token spend when something goes wrong. The lesson generalizes: agent reliability problems are usually architecture problems wearing an AI costume.

Read if you sell or evaluate agent tooling and want a believable, specific failure story rather than a vendor pitch; skip if you only care about banking-specific outcomes.

AI in Banking

Addressing AI-enabled cybersecurity threats (Letter SSM-2026-0301 to significant institutions)

ECB Banking Supervisionpublished 2026-07docs15 minworking

The ECB just sent its first letter to banks in over four years, and it's not a new AI rulebook — it's an instruction to fold AI-accelerated cyber threats into resilience programs banks already run under existing EU digital-operational-resilience rules. Every euro-area significant institution now owes its supervisory team a board-owned action plan by October 31, 2026, with a cross-bank comparison to follow. The message: frontier AI has shortened the gap between a discovered vulnerability and an actual exploit, so existing risk programs have to explicitly account for that speed.

Read if you sell into EU banks and need a real date to anchor a compliance-driven conversation; skip if your book is US-only.

Fiserv Launches agentOS: The Operating System for Agentic AI in Banking

Fiserv, Inc.published 2026-05docs10 minworking

Fiserv is packaging agent governance — identity-bound execution, policy enforcement, observability, audit trails — as built-in infrastructure rather than something each bank has to assemble, and launching it with a marketplace of Fiserv-built and third-party agents (OpenAI and AWS among the launch partners). For the thousands of mid-sized banks and credit unions running on Fiserv's core, payments, and servicing rails, this means agent governance arrives pre-installed instead of requiring a build-out. That materially changes what a smaller institution needs to buy versus what it already gets by staying on the platform.

Read if you sell orchestration, governance, or observability tooling to community banks or credit unions; skip if your accounts are large banks running their own stacks.
