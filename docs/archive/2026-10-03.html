Today's four pieces split cleanly into two halves: the agentic research explains mechanically why multi-agent pipelines fail and why adding reviewers or retries doesn't fix it, while the banking research gives a real vendor-vs-reality case study plus an EU regulatory timeline update that directly affects credit-decisioning AI projects.

Agentic AI

From Spark to Fire: Modeling and Mitigating Error Cascades in LLM-Based Multi-Agent Collaboration

arXiv (Xie, Zhu, Zhang, Zhu, Ye, Qi, Chen, Zhou)published 2026-03paper35 minworking

Errors in multi-agent pipelines don't just sit there — they spread through message dependencies into system-wide false consensus, and across six major frameworks (LangChain, MetaGPT, LangGraph, CrewAI, AutoGen, CAMEL) a single injected error reached near-total infection in most setups. The uncomfortable finding: reviewer or QA agents don't catch this because they get infected by the same false consensus too. What actually worked was tracking where each claim originated at the message level, which raised defense success from 0.32 to over 0.89 — far more effective than trusting any one agent's validation role.

Read if you need a rigorous answer for why more agents or a 'reviewer' role can make a pipeline worse, not better; skip if you just need the one-line version.

OrchestraBench: Evaluating Multi-Agent Orchestration Failure Modes, Recovery, and Decomposition Quality

arXiv (Chen, Gu, Vidra, Setty, Zheng)published 2026-08paper30 minworking

Not all agent failures are equal, and the split doesn't move with which model you use. Tool faults recover fully, ambiguous delegation only partially, and three latent or semantic failure modes never recover — even under top-tier models. Worse, just retrying doesn't fix the latent failures, it reproduces them and delays detection, so real containment needs explicit failure attribution, not more attempts.

Read if you'll be asked 'can it recover from its own mistakes' in a pilot review; skip if you only need the headline stat.

AI in Banking

AI shifts mainframe modernization strategy

CIO Divepublished 2026-07article7 minworking

Morgan Stanley's own in-house AI platform — not a third-party coding agent — has modernized more than 17 million lines of COBOL, Natural, and PERL into Java and Python, saving developers over a million hours, by having AI reverse-engineer the old code into a clear design first rather than auto-translating it line by line. A bank technology executive called a vendor's claim of fully automated COBOL modernization 'laughable.'

Read if you're pitching mainframe modernization to a bank and need a credible, audited counter-example to vendor claims; skip if you already know the DevGen.AI story.

EU AI Act Omnibus Agreement — Postponed High-Risk Deadlines and Other Key Changes

Gibson Dunnpublished 2026-05article9 minworking

The EU AI Act's toughest obligations for high-risk systems — including credit scoring, recruitment, and border control tools — were pushed back over a year, to December 2, 2027. But that delay doesn't cover everything: the Article 50 transparency obligations remain on schedule, taking effect August 2, 2026.

Read if you talk to bank compliance teams about AI Act deadlines; skip if your deals are entirely outside the EU.
