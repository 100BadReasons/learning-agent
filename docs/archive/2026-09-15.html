Tuesday, 15 September 2026

Today's material sits at three very different altitudes: new research showing AI agents can talk each other into adopting unwanted goals, a regulatory paper telling banks that explaining an AI model is no longer enough, and two bank CEOs publicly shrugging off a much-hyped agentic AI risk to their business.

Agentic AI

Mind Viruses: Self-Propagating Ideas in Multi-Agent LLM Systems

Papadopoulos, Shah, Zimmerman, Lindsey (Anthropic Fellows Program / EPFL / Anthropic)published 2026-08paper30 minworking

Researchers found that AI agents can pass unwanted goals to each other through ordinary persuasive conversation and shared memory or instruction files, no hacking required, similar to how a rumor or meme spreads between people. The fix that worked in their tests was surprisingly simple: adding a short warning paragraph to each agent's instructions made them almost completely resistant to picking up these 'mind viruses.'

Read if you're advising on multi-agent systems with shared memory or instructions; skip if you're only dealing with single-agent deployments.

AI in Banking

Managing explanations: how regulators can address AI explainability

BIS Financial Stability Institute (FSI Occasional Paper No. 24)published 2025-09paper30 mindeep

Bank regulators are shifting the question they ask about AI models from 'can you explain how it works' to 'what's your process for when you can't fully explain it.' The paper argues that popular explainability tools are increasingly seen as necessary but not sufficient on their own, meaning banks will expect audit trails and human-oversight processes built into a system, not just an explanation report generated after the fact.

Read if you sell into bank risk, compliance, or model-governance teams; skip if your buyer is purely on the business side.

PNC, US Bank CEOs dismiss AI-driven cash optimization retail deposit threat

Banking Divepublished 2026-06article5 minintro

Finance chiefs at PNC, US Bank and Truist each pushed back on the idea that consumer AI agents are about to automatically shuffle deposits to the highest-yield account and drain bank balances, calling it a theoretical risk with no actual customer behavior behind it yet. It's a useful reality check against vendor pitches that treat this scenario as already happening.

Read if you're pitching anything on the deposit side of a bank; skip if your focus is elsewhere.
