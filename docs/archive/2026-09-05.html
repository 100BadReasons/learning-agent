Agentic AI

Agents

Chip Huyenpublished 2025-01article35 minworking

An agent's real capability comes down to two things: the set of tools it's allowed to call, and how well it can plan a sequence of steps using them. Most of the hard engineering work isn't in the underlying model at all — it's in deciding what tools to expose and constraining how the agent is allowed to use them. That reframing matters because it means 'agent' isn't a marketing feature, it's an architecture decision with real limits.

Santander and Mastercard complete Europe's first live end-to-end payment executed by an AI agent

Banco Santander (press release)published 2026-03article5 minintro

A top-20 global bank ran an AI-agent-initiated payment through its actual live payments infrastructure, not an isolated test environment, using tokenized agent-payment rails with a separate orchestrator layer in between. The point of the exercise wasn't proving the transaction could happen technically — that's the easy part — it was validating that the operational and control framework around it holds up under real regulatory conditions.

AI and the future of retail financial services (The Mills Review)

UK Financial Conduct Authoritypublished 2026-07docs35 mindeep

A UK financial regulator, not an industry group, has published the first major study on how agentic AI should be governed in retail financial services — and it's proposing new supervisory machinery, including a trusted-identity framework for agents and an autonomy-spectrum model for who's accountable when one acts. Its own verdict is that the existing consumer-protection and management-accountability regime is 'broadly sound' but under strain, and its top near-term move is a 3-6 month review of whether general-purpose AI providers that influence financial decisions but sit outside the regulated perimeter need to be brought inside it.
