OpenAI single-agent LLM architecture reduces computational overhead relative to multi-agent orchestration in a simulated mars rover decision-support benchmark

Frontiers in Robotics and AI (Sanabria, 2026)published 2026-07paper25 minworking

A controlled benchmark pitted a single AI agent against a team of specialized agents on a decision task where all the needed information already fit in one input. The specialist-team setup didn't produce better decisions — it just used substantially more compute and took longer to respond. The takeaway isn't 'multi-agent is bad,' it's that splitting an agent into multiple specialists is a cost you have to justify with the task's actual complexity, not a default upgrade.

Read if you need a straight answer for 'should we architect this as multiple agents?'; skip if your customer's use case is already known to be complex and multi-step.

AI in Banking

Research Note: AI in UK financial services

FCA / Bank of Englandpublished 2025-11docs20 minworking

Surveying 118 UK banks, insurers, and market infrastructure firms, the regulator found adoption is broad but shallow: foundation models are only 17% of AI use cases, a mere 2% of automated decisions run fully autonomously, and just 34% of firms say they completely understand the AI systems they've already deployed. The gap between 'we've deployed it' and 'we understand it' is the actual risk here, not the underlying technology.

Read if you sell into UK or EU-regulated financial firms and want real numbers instead of vendor talking points; skip if your accounts are purely US retail banks.

Communicating Credit Risk with Large Language Models: Evaluation of Explanations from Standard and Alternative Data-Based Models

arXivpublished 2026-08paper35 mindeep

Tested on real mortgage data with actual credit risk professionals, this study found that AI-generated plain-language explanations of credit decisions are only as good as the underlying evidence method feeding them (SHAP-style feature attribution vs. graph-based explainers vs. combined approaches) — swapping which language model narrates the explanation barely matters. Critically, credit professionals graded these explanations by stricter evidentiary standards, while non-experts were more easily persuaded by confident, clear writing regardless of whether it was actually well-supported.

Read if you're pitching 'explainable AI' for credit decisioning to a risk or compliance audience; skip if your deal doesn't touch model explainability.
