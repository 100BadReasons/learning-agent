Monday, 5 October 2026

Today's material is narrow but substantive: two Microsoft research pieces make the case that multi-agent systems fail in ways single-agent testing simply doesn't catch, with no banking-specific research to ground it in a buyer's world.

Agentic AI

Red-teaming a network of agents: Understanding what breaks when AI agents interact at scale

Microsoft Researchpublished 2026-04article18 minworking

When researchers tested AI agents that talk to each other rather than individually, safety that held at the single-agent level completely broke down. A single malicious message passed from one agent to another extracted private data at every step it traveled through, and even pulled unrelated agents into the breach. This shows that multi-agent systems carry a distinct category of risk that simply doesn't appear when you test agents one at a time.

Read if you're explaining to a customer why a multi-agent architecture needs its own security review, not just a sign-off on each individual agent in it. Skip if you only need a general summary of agent risk.

Updating the taxonomy of failure modes in agentic AI systems: What a year of red teaming taught us

Microsoft AI Red Teampublished 2026-06article20 minworking

A year of red-teaming against production agent systems has forced a bigger, more precise catalogue of what can go wrong. The update adds seven new failure categories, including attackers hiding malicious instructions inside the plain-language descriptions tools use to advertise their own capabilities, and new visual attacks targeting agents that operate a screen or interface directly. These risks only became visible once agents were handling real tool integrations and real interfaces at scale, not in lab testing.

Read if you need sharper, more credible vocabulary for discussing agent risk categories with a security-conscious buyer. Skip if you aren't yet having agent-deployment conversations with customers.
