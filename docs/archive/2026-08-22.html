Three pieces today triangulate the same practical question from different angles: a math paper on exactly when adding more agents stops helping, a reality check on what AI can and can't yet do in mainframe modernization, and a named bank's live agentic deployment with real numbers to compare against the hype in the other two.

Agentic AI

Phase Transition for Budgeted Multi-Agent Synergy

Bang Liu, Linglong Kong, Jian Pei (Université de Montréal/Mila, U Alberta, Duke)published 2026-01paper30 mindeep

Under a fixed compute budget, throwing more agents at a problem only beats a single well-run agent when a measurable property of how the agents are organized exceeds how well that single agent scales on its own. Below that threshold, coordination overhead, lossy messages between agents, and correlated failures mean more agents produces flat or worse results — not a diminishing-but-positive return, a genuine reversal. The paper gives this a precise, calculable cutoff rather than leaving it as a vague intuition.

Read if you need a rigorous answer for 'why not just add more agents' in a sales or architecture conversation; skip if you just need the practical bottom line, not the math.

AI in Banking

Summer Mentorship 2026: Discovery Is Not Migration — Where AI Actually Stands for Mainframe Modernization

Open Mainframe Project (Linux Foundation)published 2026-07article10 minworking

The credible, currently-working use of AI in mainframe modernization is exploring and documenting what old COBOL code actually does — not automatically rewriting or migrating it. Even the incumbent vendor's own tooling is sequenced to explain the legacy logic first, before any conversion is attempted, and other vendors with a commercial reason to talk up automated migration are instead publicly warning that AI-converted code can run identically but still be no easier to maintain. This is the piece that reconciles the two loud, contradictory 2026 headlines about mainframe AI you've probably already seen.

Read if you're selling anything into a bank with a mainframe estate; skip if your accounts have none.

OCBC harnesses agentic AI for faster onboarding of wealthy customers (HELIOS)

OCBC Holdingspublished 2026-07article6 minintro

OCBC's private bank is running an agentic onboarding system live in three markets, with over 100 relationship managers using it and roughly 50 clients fully onboarded so far. It front-loads KYC data gathering and relationship-network mapping before the relationship manager ever meets the prospect, cutting median account-opening time from about six weeks to 15 business days — while humans still make every final call. It's a rare case of a named institution publishing checkable production numbers rather than a vendor's projected range.

Read if you need a concrete, named reference case for agentic AI in wealth onboarding; skip if your buyer is retail banking, not private wealth.
